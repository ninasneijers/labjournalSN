Final Project - Theory

by: Nina Sneijers

1 Theory

Homophily

Homophily is a concept that addresses the fact that “a contact between similar people occurs at a higher rate than among dissimilar people” (Tolsma and Hofstra 2022). It thus is about if we share similar characteristics, such as gender or ethnicity, we are more likely to befriend one another. Studies show that this is also likely to occur on a socioeconomic level, and that students are more likely to connect with students that have the same socio-economic status (Zwier and Geven 2023). Socioeconomic status is both about educational level and income-level, but this study solely looks at parental educational level. In this study, the selection process that is expected to take place is that students are expected to befriend other students whose parents have the same educational level, even when controlling for gender and ethnicity. On a macro-level, I expect to see that the network is segregated based on this characteristic, and on a micro-level, I expect that segregation is especially taking place when looking at reciprocated ties, so whose friendships are answered.

Hypothesis 1: The MLC network is segregated on parental education level.

Hypothesis 2: Reciprocated ties are more likely to exist among students with parents with the same educational level

Redirected ties

That person A sees person B as a friend, does not necessarily have to mean that person B sees person A as a friend as well. However, one can imagine that if a friendship is answered, this creates more social satisfaction than if ties are only sent, but not answered. Therefore, it is expected that:

Hypothesis 3 Reciprocated ties positively affect students’ social satisfaction

Cultural values

The family one grows up in influences the values one has and what world one is prepared for. Stephens et al. (2014) show that there is a difference in how working class families and middle class families prepare their children for the world, they, in fact, prepare their children for different types of worlds. First generation students are often guided by more interdependent motives than continuing-generation students and these norms remain the same over time in college (Phillips et al. 2020). Not having to navigate between different cultural contexts is likely to be less demanding for students, therefore, it is expected that if students befriend people who have parents with a similar educational level, they have probably similar expectations in terms of friendships and it increases their social satisfaction.

Hypothesis 4: If reciprocated ties occur between students with parents with the same educational level, this positively affects students’ social satisfaction
