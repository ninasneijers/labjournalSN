For this week I prepared my presentation, which includes my final research question and hypotheses, the variables and effects I included and two RSiena models. Throughout the presentation I have put the questions that I have so I will address them when I get to it.

1 Final Research Questions:

Descriptive research question 1: “To what extent do reciprocated friendships among students with parents with the same educational level occur?”

H1: Reciprocated friendships are more likely to occur among students with parents with the same educational level

Descriptive research question 2: “To what extent do reciprocated friendships affect students’ social satisfaction throughout the year?”

H2: Reciprocated friendships positively affect students’ social satisfaction throughout the year

Explanatory “To what extent do reciprocated friendships among students with parents with the same education level affect their social satisfaction throughout the year?”

H3: Reciprocated friendships among students with parents with the same educational level increase the positive effect of reciprocated friendships on social satisfaction

Questions and comments:

  • Are my hypotheses okay formulated like this? I am a bit in doubt about the last one, if I formulated that correctly.

  • For H1, I will be controlling for gender and ethnicity

  • For H2 and H3, I will be controlling for an assimilation effect, which is about that alter and ego mimic each other. Is it correct if I explain it like this, or should I formulate that differently?

2 Variables of interest

Ego-level:

  • Educational level of parents (high/low)

  • Gender (control)

  • Ethnicity (control)

  • Social satisfaction (dependent variable)

On dyadic level:

  • social satisfaction assimilation

  • dyadic similarity based on gender, ethnicity and parental education level

On a network level:

  • Homogeneity based on parental education level

Question: Have I understood it correct like this or should I write it up differently? I feel like I might be missing some things.

3 Included RSiena Effects

Structural effects:

  • Density effect (density)

  • Reciprocity effect (recip)

  • Transitivity effect (transTrip)

  • Network-Isolate Effect (isolateNet)

  • In-degree related popularity effect (inPop)

  • Out-degree activity (outAct)

I based this on [@FRANKEN2023] and Chapter 7 of SNAS [@SNASS]. Are these the ones that should always be included?

For hypothesis 1:

  • sameX > defines number of ties of i to all actors j with the same covariate

  • sameXRecip > defines the number of reciprocated ties of i to all actors j with the same covariate

I will do this in my first model for only parental education level and in my second model gender and ethnicity will be included as control variables.

For hypothesis 2:

  • Effects of H1 –> I have to include effects of H1 as well right?
  • RecipDeg > Reciprocated degree effect on behaviour (social satisfaction)
  • avRecAlt > Average reciprocated alter effect, so this takes into account how i’s behaviour is affected by the average behaviour of his reciprocated alters

The third model will include only the RecipDeg and it will then be compared with the fourth model that includes how ego is affected by behaviour of alter.

For hypothesis 3:

  • Effects of H1 + H2

  • avSameXRecAlt > effect on behaviour of i as influenced by the reciprocated ties of i that share the same covariate

  • avRecAltSameX > average reciprocated alter for same covariate, which looks at the effect of the average behaviour of alter to whom i has a reciprocated tie and where they also share the same covariate value (so in this case their parents have the same educational level)

The fifth model will include avSameXRecAlt and the sixth model will be compared with this to see if the effect (if it is present) would still be present when the average behaviour of alter is taken into account.

fpackage.check <- function(packages) {
    lapply(packages, FUN = function(x) {
        if (!require(x, character.only = TRUE)) {
            install.packages(x, dependencies = TRUE)
            library(x, character.only = TRUE)
        }
    })
}

This null model has a very high convergence ratio of 20.96. I am not sure whether this is expected or very strange. The RSiena model results indicate that the network is sparse, as a negative density effect is seen (b = -0.74, SE = 0.4). The students overall thus form relatively few ties within their community. However, ties seem to be relatively reciprocal, because the effect is positive (b = 2.14, SE = .26).

(Now follows how I think inPop and outAct should be interpreted). Relatively few nodes seem to receive many degrees, as indegree popularity is relatively low and negative (b = 0.85, SE = 0.19) and out-degree activity also seems to be relatively low (b = -0.08, SE = 0.05). I think this can be explained by the high ratio of network isolates (b = 636.65, SE = 230.39).
